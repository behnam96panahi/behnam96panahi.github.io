About

I build small pieces of human heart in the lab. Starting from induced pluripotent stem cells, I differentiate them into atrial heart muscle and fibroblasts, then 3D-bioprint them into tissue that beats — and, when I want it to, scars.

That scarring is the point. Atrial fibrosis drives atrial fibrillation, the most common heart-rhythm disorder in the world, and it is very hard to study in a dish that is flat and plastic. By tuning bioink stiffness, print geometry and cell ratios, I make tissue that recreates the fibrotic heart closely enough to test drugs against it.

My training sits between chemical engineering, materials and cell biology, which means I am equally comfortable formulating a hydrogel, running a differentiation, and putting the result under a confocal. I care most about work that leaves the bench: models a company can actually screen with.

Research

Three projects, one question: can we make a heart honest enough to test on?

Each of these tackles a different failure of conventional models — geometry, disease state, or oxygen supply.

2022 — present · PhD · UBC

HeartPrint: personalized cardiac tissue for drug development

Patient-derived stem cells become beating tissue with a printed architecture. I optimize hydrogel bioinks, print patterns and matrix stiffness to push the cells toward a mature, adult-like phenotype — the state where a drug response actually means something.

  • hiPSC-CM
  • CELLINK BIO X6
  • Bioink rheology
  • Tissue maturation

Ongoing · Presented at ISSCR & TERMIS

Printing fibrosis to understand atrial fibrillation

Fibrosis is not just extra collagen — it is fibroblasts switching identity under mechanical and chemical stress, then rewiring how electrical signals travel. I build tissue with tunable stiffness and controlled fibroblast content, then measure what changes: conduction, contraction, arrhythmia susceptibility, and drug responsiveness.

  • Atrial fibrosis
  • Myofibroblast activation
  • Electrophysiology
  • Multiparametric modeling

2019 — 2021 · MSc · Sharif University of Technology

Microchanneled cardiac patches for oxygen delivery

Thick engineered tissue suffocates from the inside out. I designed and fabricated patches threaded with microchannels that mimic vascular networks, and used CFD simulation to work out which geometries keep oxygen — and cells — alive deep inside the construct.

  • Microfluidics
  • CFD simulation
  • SolidWorks
  • Cardiac regeneration
